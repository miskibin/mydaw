I am building a small built-in software synthesizer for a DAW (Rust audio engine, real time, 48 kHz, block-based, no allocation in the audio thread; UI in a web view). Target users: beginner–intermediate producers making hip-hop, trap, pop, R&B, EDM and lo-fi. The synth must be SIMPLE (one screen, about 25–35 knobs) but its PRESETS must sound modern and professional out of the box. Please research and write a technical report I can implement from without guessing. Cite primary sources (papers, DAFx/AES/ICMC proceedings, author blogs of known DSP engineers, official manuals of well-known synths, open-source code with licence) for every algorithm, and mark anything you could not verify.

1. Architecture choice. Compare, for this goal, (a) a classic subtractive design (2–3 oscillators + sub + noise, multimode filter, 2–3 envelopes, 2 LFOs, small mod matrix), (b) a wavetable design, (c) a hybrid (virtual-analog oscillators + a few wavetables). Look at what simple but well-loved synths ship with (e.g. Serum's basic layout, Vital, Sylenth1, Massive, Spire, FL Studio's 3x Osc and Sytrus, Ableton Analog/Wavetable, Logic Retro Synth, Arturia Pigments basics, u-he Zebra/Diva overview) and recommend one design with an exact parameter list (name, range, default, curve/skew, and whether it is smoothed per sample or per block).

2. Oscillators. Band-limited algorithms with concrete formulas and CPU cost: PolyBLEP/PolyBLAMP, minBLEP, BLIT, mip-mapped wavetables (how many mip levels, table size, interpolation, how to avoid clicks when switching tables). Supersaw/unison: how many voices, detune curve (e.g. Adam Szabo's analysis of the JP-8000 supersaw), stereo spread, phase randomisation, and how to keep mono compatibility. Hard sync, FM/PM (phase modulation, linear vs exponential), pulse width modulation. Recommended oversampling (if any) and its filters.

3. Filters. Zero-delay-feedback / TPT state-variable filter (Vadim Zavalishin, "The Art of VA Filter Design", Andrew Simper/Cytomic technical papers): exact equations for SVF LP/BP/HP/notch, a 4-pole ladder (Huovilainen, Zavalishin, Välimäki), stable self-oscillation, resonance compensation, nonlinearities (tanh placement) and their aliasing, cutoff modulation at audio rate, key tracking, envelope amount. Give stable coefficient update rules for fast modulation.

4. Envelopes, LFOs, modulation. ADSR curve shapes that sound "analog" (exponential segments, attack overshoot target such as 1.3), retrigger/legato behaviour, velocity mapping, LFO shapes, tempo sync, smoothing, a minimal mod matrix (sources × destinations) that covers 90 % of presets.

5. Voices. Polyphony count, voice stealing policies, mono/legato with glide (portamento curves, constant-time vs constant-rate), 808-style glide for trap basses, note-on click avoidance, denormals, per-voice vs global filter.

6. Built-in effects worth including inside the synth (chorus, unison spread, simple delay, reverb send, drive/saturation, OTT-style multiband compression) — which ones matter most for preset quality and the simplest good algorithms for each.

7. Presets — the most important part. For each of these categories give 3–5 concrete presets with FULL parameter values on your recommended architecture (not vague descriptions): 808 / sub bass, reese bass, pluck, lead (supersaw and square), pad (warm, airy), keys (e-piano-ish, organ-ish), bell/mallet, brass/strings-ish, arp, lo-fi/vintage, FX/riser. Explain the sound-design principles behind each (what makes a modern pluck or a trap 808 sound right: pitch envelope depth/time, saturation, envelope times, filter env amount). Cite tutorials or manuals from known sound designers where possible. Include loudness targets so presets are level-matched (e.g. peak and LUFS per preset at velocity 100) and safe headroom defaults.

8. Validation. How to measure that the synth is good: aliasing measurement (sweep tests, SNR of aliasing), CPU per voice targets, click tests, comparison against reference synths, blind listening (MUSHRA-style) protocol for presets, and any public datasets of synth sounds with parameters (e.g. NSynth, Synth1 preset datasets, "Diva/Dexed" preset corpora, InverSynth, sound-matching papers) that could help design or validate presets.

9. Licensing. Which algorithms or open-source implementations (Vital is GPL-3, Surge is GPL-3, Dexed GPL-3, Helm GPL-3, etc.) I must NOT copy code from for a closed-source product, and which are permissively licensed (MIT/BSD/Apache, e.g. specific Rust crates like fundsp) or patent-free. Note any patents (e.g. on ladder filters or specific oscillators) that still matter.

Output format: a structured report with sections 1–9, formulas in plain text or LaTeX, pseudo-code for each DSP block, a final parameter table, a preset table (preset × parameter values), and a short "implementation order" list. Prefer concrete numbers over adjectives.
